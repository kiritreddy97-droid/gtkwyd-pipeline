## Slicing Wood
In most factories, giant pine or poplar logs are peeled into razor-thin wooden sheets.

## Chopping Sticks
High-speed chopping blades quickly slice these thin sheets into millions of tiny matchsticks.

## Wax Coating
The blank wooden sticks are soaked in paraffin wax so the flame burns smoothly.

Next, one end of each stick is dipped into a mixture containing potassium chlorate.

## Striking Strip
The side of the cardboard box is coated with red phosphorus and powdered glass.
[[matchbox striking surface]] [[red phosphorus matchbox]]
{{A 3D render of a mechanical roller applying a dark brown gritty stripe to a cardboard matchbox.}} {{A extreme close-up of the textured brown striking strip on the side of a matchbox.}}

## Friction Heat
Striking the match creates friction, converting red phosphorus into white phosphorus gas instantly.
[[striking match friction]] [[matchstick ignition close up]]
{{A 3D render showing microscopic friction particles igniting between match head and box strip.}} {{A close-up cutaway of a match head rubbing the striking strip with smoke particles forming.}}

## Safe Ignition
This chemical reaction ignites the head safely, making accidental fires virtually impossible.
