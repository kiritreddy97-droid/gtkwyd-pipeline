## A colony acts as one
A single ant follows simple rules and reacts to scent trails. Add thousands
together and the colony solves problems no ant understands, like finding the
shortest path to food.
[[ants marching trail]] [[ant colony close up]]

## Enormous combined weight
Recent estimates put the world's ant population around 20 quadrillion. Their
total dry weight rivals that of all wild birds and mammals put together.
[[ants swarming ground]] [[forest floor ants]]

## Farmers and herders
Leafcutter ants do not eat the leaves they carry. They use them to grow a fungus
that feeds the colony, a form of farming older than human agriculture.
[[leafcutter ants carrying leaves]] [[ant fungus garden]]

## Living bridges
Army ants link their bodies to form bridges and rafts. During floods a colony
can roll into a living raft that floats for weeks with the queen protected
inside.
[[army ants bridge]] [[ant raft water]]

## Built-in pedometer
Desert ants find their way home by counting steps and tracking the sun. In
experiments, ants on stilts overshot the nest, and ants with shortened legs
stopped short.
[[desert ant sand]] [[single ant walking]]

## Some never sleep as we do
Many ants take hundreds of tiny power naps a day rather than one long sleep, so
the colony always has workers awake.
