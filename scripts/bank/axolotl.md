## Regrowing limbs
An axolotl can regrow an amputated leg, complete with bone, muscle, and nerves,
in a few weeks, and can do it over and over without a scar.
[[axolotl close up]] [[axolotl swimming tank]]

## More than limbs
Axolotls can also repair parts of the heart, spinal cord, and brain. Scientists
study them to understand why mammals cannot do the same.
[[axolotl gills]] [[laboratory axolotl research]]

## Forever young
Most salamanders lose their gills and move to land as adults. The axolotl keeps
its gills and stays aquatic its whole life, a condition called neoteny.
[[axolotl feathery gills]] [[axolotl on gravel]]

## Nearly gone in the wild
Wild axolotls live only in a few canals and lakes near Mexico City and are
critically endangered, even though hundreds of thousands live in labs and homes.
[[xochimilco canals]] [[axolotl in pond]]

## A giant genome
The axolotl genome is about ten times larger than the human genome, which made
it very hard to sequence.
[[dna sequence illustration]] [[axolotl portrait]]

## They eat by suction
An axolotl has weak jaws and feeds by rapidly opening its mouth to vacuum in
prey along with a gulp of water.
