## The longest commute
The Arctic tern breeds near the North Pole and winters near Antarctica. Its
zig-zag yearly route can top 70,000 kilometres, the longest migration known.

## A magnetic sense
Birds can detect Earth's magnetic field. Evidence points to specialised cells
and light-sensitive proteins in the eye that let some birds effectively see the
field as a pattern.

## Star maps
Young songbirds raised under a planetarium sky learn to orient by the rotation of
the stars around the celestial pole, and use it on their first night flights.
[[night sky stars]] [[bird silhouette dusk]]

## Sleeping on the wing
Frigatebirds and swifts can stay airborne for months. They take brief seconds-
long naps in flight, sometimes resting only half the brain at a time.
[[frigatebird gliding]] [[swift in flight]]

## Non-stop ocean crossings
The bar-tailed godwit flies from Alaska to New Zealand without landing, up to
around 11 days and 11,000 kilometres, burning half its body weight.
[[godwit shorebird]] [[open ocean sky]]

## Inherited directions
A first-time migrant with no guide still flies the right way. The direction and
rough distance are written into its genes.
