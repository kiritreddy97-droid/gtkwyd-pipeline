## Not cosmic vacuum cleaners
A black hole only pulls things in if they get close. Replace the Sun with a black
hole of the same mass and Earth's orbit would not change at all.
[[black hole illustration]] [[galaxy space stars]]

## The point of no return
The event horizon is not a surface but a boundary. Once light itself cannot
escape from inside it, no information can get back out.
[[black hole event horizon art]] [[light bending space]]

## Time slows at the edge
To a faraway observer, an object falling in appears to slow down and fade near
the horizon, never quite crossing it, because time runs slower in strong
gravity.
[[warped spacetime grid]] [[distant galaxy]]

## They can spin and ring
Black holes rotate, some near the speed of light, dragging space around with
them. When two merge, they briefly ring like a struck bell, sending out
gravitational waves.
[[two black holes merging]] [[gravitational waves illustration]]

## The first photo
In 2019 the Event Horizon Telescope linked observatories across Earth to image
the shadow of the black hole in galaxy M87, 55 million light years away.
[[event horizon telescope image]] [[radio telescope dish]]

## They slowly evaporate
Theory predicts black holes leak a faint radiation and shrink over time. For a
star-sized one this would take vastly longer than the current age of the
universe.
