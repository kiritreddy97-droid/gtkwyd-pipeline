## Rock versus ice
Asteroids are mostly rock and metal and orbit mainly between Mars and Jupiter.
Comets are ice, dust, and frozen gases and come from the cold outer solar system.
[[asteroid in space]] [[comet with tail]]

## The tail is made near the Sun
Far out, a comet is a dark frozen lump. As it nears the Sun the ice turns to gas,
releasing dust and forming a glowing head and tails hundreds of thousands of
kilometres long.
[[comet approaching sun]] [[bright comet night sky]]

## Tails point away from the Sun
Sunlight and the solar wind push the comet's gas and dust outward, so the tail
always streams away from the Sun, even as the comet heads back out and travels
tail-first.
[[comet tail diagram]] [[comet over landscape]]

## Meteors are the leftovers
When Earth passes through the dusty trail left by a comet, those particles burn
up in the atmosphere as a meteor shower.
[[meteor shower night]] [[shooting star streak]]

## Some visitors never return
Many comets loop back on regular schedules, like Halley's every 76 years. Others
are flung onto paths that leave the solar system forever after one pass.
[[halley comet illustration]] [[deep space comet]]

## They may have delivered water
One idea for how Earth got much of its water and organic molecules is that
comets and water-rich asteroids brought them during the young solar system's
heavy bombardment.
