## They know your face
In studies, crows learned to recognise specific human faces linked to a threat,
scolded those people on sight, and passed the reaction to other crows that had
never seen the person.

## Tool users
New Caledonian crows shape hooks from twigs and leaves to fish grubs out of wood.
Some bend wire into hooks, a skill once thought unique to humans and great apes.

## Planning ahead
Ravens will pick and keep a tool now for a task they know is coming later, and
will pass up a small treat for a better one in the future.

## Traffic tricks
Crows have been seen dropping hard nuts onto roads for cars to crack, then
waiting for a gap in traffic to collect the pieces.
[[crow on road]] [[crow eating nut]]

## Funerals
When crows find a dead crow, they gather and call loudly around it. Researchers
think they are learning about danger rather than mourning.

## Gift givers
Crows that are regularly fed by a person have been reported bringing back small
shiny objects, dropping them where the food appears.
