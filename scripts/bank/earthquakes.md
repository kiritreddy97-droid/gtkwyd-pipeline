## The scale is not linear
Each whole step up the moment magnitude scale means roughly 32 times more energy
released. A magnitude 7 is not a bit worse than a 6, it is about a thousand times
more energetic than a 5.

## Two kinds of wave
An earthquake sends out fast pressure waves and slower side-to-side waves. The
gap between them lets seismologists work out how far away the quake was.

## The ground can turn to liquid
In loose, wet soil, strong shaking can cause liquefaction, where the ground
briefly behaves like a fluid and buildings sink or tip.

## The biggest ever recorded
The 1960 earthquake in Chile reached about magnitude 9.5, the largest measured
with modern instruments. It shifted the ground for hundreds of kilometres.
[[chile coastline]] [[tsunami wave illustration]]

## They can shorten the day
Very large quakes redistribute Earth's mass slightly, changing its spin. The 2011
Japan quake is estimated to have shortened the day by about a microsecond.

## Animals sometimes react early
There are many reports of unusual animal behaviour before quakes, possibly from
sensing early tremors or small changes in the ground, though it is not reliable
enough to predict them.
