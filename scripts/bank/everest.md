## The top was once a seabed
The summit rock is limestone formed on an ocean floor. It holds fossils of sea
creatures, lifted nearly nine kilometres up as India collided with Asia.

## It is still growing
The collision that built the Himalayas continues, pushing Everest up by a few
millimetres a year, though erosion and earthquakes complicate the exact change.
[[everest peak clouds]] [[tectonic plates illustration]]

## Not the tallest from base to top
Measured from its underwater base, Mauna Kea in Hawaii is taller. From the centre
of the Earth, a peak in Ecuador wins because the planet bulges at the equator.
[[mauna kea hawaii]] [[chimborazo ecuador]]

## The death zone
Above about 8,000 metres there is roughly a third of the oxygen available at sea
level. The body slowly deteriorates and cannot acclimatise, so climbers must move
fast.
[[climbers on ridge everest]] [[oxygen mask mountaineer]]

## A traffic jam at the top
On good-weather days, hundreds of climbers may attempt the summit at once,
creating long queues on the final ridge.
[[queue of climbers everest]] [[everest base camp tents]]

## The jet stream scours the peak
For much of the year, winds over 160 kilometres per hour blast the summit. Climbs
target short calm windows, usually in May.
