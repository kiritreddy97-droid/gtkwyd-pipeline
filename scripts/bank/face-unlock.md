## More than a camera
A basic front camera alone can be fooled by a photograph. Secure face unlock
systems add extra hardware specifically to prevent that trick.
[[phone face unlock scan]] [[smartphone front camera]]

## Projecting invisible dots
Many phones project thousands of infrared dots onto your face in a pattern
invisible to human eyes, then read how that pattern warps across your
features to build a 3D depth map.
[[infrared dot projection illustration]] [[face scan technology]]

## A map, not a picture
Like a fingerprint scanner, the system converts that 3D shape into a
mathematical model and stores it securely on the device, not as a photo
anyone could recognise if it leaked.
[[face mapping animation]] [[secure data illustration]]

## Checking it is really you
Some systems also use an infrared camera to check for the natural heat and
texture of real skin, which is why a printed photo or a flat screen image
typically fails to unlock the phone.
[[infrared face detection]] [[phone unlock failed]]

## It adapts to you over time
Face unlock systems gradually update their stored model to account for
glasses, facial hair, or ageing, comparing new scans against a range of
recent successful unlocks.
[[person wearing glasses phone]] [[face recognition update]]

## Works best in the dark too
Because it relies on projected infrared light rather than visible light,
face unlock often works reliably in a dark room, unlike a normal photograph.
