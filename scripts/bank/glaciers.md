## Ice that flows
Under its own weight, glacier ice deforms and slides. A glacier moves downhill
from a few centimetres to a few metres a day, faster in the middle than at the
edges.

## They bulldoze the land
A glacier freezes onto rock, plucks pieces loose, and drags them along its base.
Those rocks act like sandpaper, grinding the bedrock beneath.
[[glacier scratched bedrock]] [[rocky glacier terminus]]

## U-shaped valleys
Rivers cut narrow V-shaped valleys. Glaciers grind out wide, flat-bottomed
U-shapes. You can often tell which one shaped a valley just by its cross-section.
[[u shaped glacial valley]] [[alpine valley landscape]]

## Fjords are drowned valleys
When a glacier carves a deep valley below sea level and then melts, the sea
floods in, creating a fjord with steep walls plunging straight into deep water.
[[norwegian fjord]] [[fjord cliffs water]]

## Erratics and rock flour
Glaciers leave huge out-of-place boulders called erratics, and grind rock into a
fine powder that turns meltwater lakes a milky turquoise.
[[glacial erratic boulder]] [[turquoise glacial lake]]

## They store ancient air
Bubbles trapped in deep glacier ice hold samples of the atmosphere from hundreds
of thousands of years ago, which scientists read from ice cores.
