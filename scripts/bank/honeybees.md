## A hive has no boss
The queen bee does not give orders. She lays eggs. Every other decision, where to
forage, when to swarm, how warm to keep the nest, comes from thousands of workers
reacting to each other with no central control.

## They vote with dances
When a swarm needs a new home, scout bees inspect sites and perform a waggle dance
to advertise the best ones. The longer and stronger the dance, the better the site.
Scouts visit rivals, and the swarm leaves once enough agree.

## A teaspoon of honey is a lifetime of work
A single worker bee produces about one twelfth of a teaspoon of honey in her whole
life. The honey in one jar represents the flights of hundreds of bees covering
thousands of miles between them.

## They see colours we cannot
Bees cannot see red, but they can see ultraviolet. Many flowers have ultraviolet
patterns, invisible to us, that act like landing strips pointing straight at the
nectar.
[[flower ultraviolet pattern]] [[bee landing on flower]]

## They keep the hive at room temperature
Brood needs to stay near 35 degrees Celsius. In summer bees fan their wings and
spread water to cool the nest. In winter they cluster and shiver their muscles to
generate heat, rotating position so no bee stays on the cold edge.

## The dance is a map
The waggle dance encodes direction and distance. The angle of the waggle relative
to straight up matches the angle of the food relative to the sun, and the duration
of the waggle tells the others how far to fly.
