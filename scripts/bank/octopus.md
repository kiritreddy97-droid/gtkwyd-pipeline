## Three hearts
An octopus has three hearts. Two pump blood through the gills, and the third
sends it around the body. That third heart stops when the octopus swims, which
is one reason they often prefer to crawl.

## Blue blood
Octopus blood is blue because it carries oxygen with a copper-based molecule
called hemocyanin instead of the iron-based hemoglobin in our red blood. It works
better in cold, low-oxygen water.

## Arms that think
About two thirds of an octopus's neurons are in its arms, not its head. Each arm
can sense and react on its own, so the animal delegates much of its control to
its limbs.

## Tasting by touch
The suckers are lined with chemical receptors, so when an octopus explores a
crack in the reef it is tasting everything it touches, not just feeling it.
[[octopus suckers macro]] [[coral reef crevice]]

## Instant camouflage
Pigment sacs called chromatophores let an octopus change colour in under a
second, and it can change skin texture too, despite almost certainly being
colour blind.
[[octopus camouflage reef]] [[octopus changing color]]

## Boneless escape artists
The only hard part of an octopus is its beak. If the beak fits through a gap, the
whole animal can squeeze through, which is how they escape sealed tanks.
