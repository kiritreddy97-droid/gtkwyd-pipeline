## Barter was never the whole story
The old tale that everyone bartered before money is oversimplified. Early societies
tracked who owed what using tallies and written records. Money mostly made those
records portable.
[[ancient market illustration]] [[old ledger book]]

## China printed it first
By around the 11th century, merchants and then the government in China were issuing
paper notes backed by deposits of coins, centuries before paper money appeared in
Europe.
[[ancient chinese paper note]] [[historical printing block]]

## Notes were receipts for gold
For a long time a banknote was a claim check. You could in principle walk into the
issuing bank and swap the paper for a fixed weight of gold or silver.
[[gold bars vault]] [[vintage bank building]]

## The link to gold was cut
Through the 20th century, countries gradually stopped promising to exchange notes
for metal. By 1971 the last major link between the US dollar and gold was ended.
[[stack of banknotes]] [[stock exchange floor historical]]

## Now value rests on trust and law
Modern money has value because governments accept it for taxes, laws require it to
be accepted for debts, and everyone expects everyone else to keep taking it.
[[modern currency notes fan]] [[people paying at shop]]

## Most money is not physical
Today the large majority of money exists only as numbers in bank computers. Physical
notes and coins are a small slice of the total.
