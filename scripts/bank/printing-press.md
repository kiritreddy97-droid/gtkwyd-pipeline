## Books were copied by hand
For centuries, every book was hand-written by a scribe, taking months or years.
Books were so rare and costly that even large libraries held only a few hundred.
[[medieval scribe manuscript]] [[old library books]]

## Movable type was the breakthrough
Around 1440 in Mainz, Johannes Gutenberg combined reusable metal letters, oil-
based ink, and a modified wine press. Pages could now be set, printed in bulk,
and reset for the next book.
[[gutenberg press replica]] [[metal movable type]]

## Printing spread explosively
Within 50 years, presses were operating in over 250 European towns and had
produced millions of books, more than all the scribes of the previous thousand
years combined.
[[early printed book pages]] [[old european town]]

## It standardised language
Printers had to pick spellings and grammar. Their choices, repeated in thousands
of copies, helped freeze local dialects into standard national languages.
[[antique printed dictionary]] [[printing workshop historical]]

## It fuelled upheaval
Cheap pamphlets let new ideas spread past the control of church and state,
feeding the Reformation and later the scientific revolution.
[[historical pamphlets]] [[printing press operators]]

## Asia printed first
Woodblock printing and even earlier movable type existed in China and Korea
centuries before Gutenberg. His system suited alphabets with few characters
especially well.
