## Older than trees
Sharks have existed for more than 400 million years. That means sharks predate
the first trees, and they have survived every mass extinction since.
[[shark swimming ocean]] [[ancient ocean illustration]]

## Electric sense
Small jelly-filled pores on a shark's snout, called ampullae of Lorenzini, detect
the faint electric fields given off by other animals. A hidden fish still gives
itself away with its heartbeat.

## Skin like sandpaper
Shark skin is covered in tiny tooth-like scales called denticles. They reduce
drag and were the inspiration for some competitive swimsuit designs.
[[shark skin texture]] [[shark gliding water]]

## Endless teeth
Many sharks grow teeth in rows that rotate forward like a conveyor belt. A single
shark can go through tens of thousands of teeth in a lifetime.
[[shark teeth close up]] [[great white shark]]

## They must keep water moving
Sharks breathe by passing water over their gills. Some can pump water while
resting, but others have to keep swimming to stay oxygenated.
[[shark swimming past camera]] [[reef sharks]]

## Most are small and harmless
Over half of shark species are under one metre long. The dwarf lantern shark
fits in a human hand, and the largest, the whale shark, eats plankton.
