## Light has a speed limit
Light travels about 300,000 kilometres per second in a vacuum. Fast, but not
instant, and nothing carrying information can go faster.
[[light beam space]] [[stars night sky]]

## The Sun is eight minutes ago
Sunlight takes just over eight minutes to cross the distance to Earth. If the Sun
vanished, we would keep seeing it, and feeling its gravity, for those eight
minutes.
[[sun in sky]] [[solar system scale]]

## The Moon is a bit over a second away
Light from the Moon reaches us in about 1.3 seconds. Radio calls to Apollo
astronauts had a noticeable delay for exactly this reason.
[[full moon detail]] [[earth and moon]]

## Nearby stars are years old
The closest star system, Alpha Centauri, is over four light years away, so its
light left before you started whatever you are doing today.
[[alpha centauri star field]] [[deep space stars]]

## Galaxies are millions of years old
The Andromeda galaxy is about 2.5 million light years away. The light hitting
your eye left it before modern humans existed.
[[andromeda galaxy]] [[telescope pointed at sky]]

## Telescopes are time machines
The most powerful telescopes catch light from galaxies over 13 billion years
old, showing the universe as it was not long after it began.
