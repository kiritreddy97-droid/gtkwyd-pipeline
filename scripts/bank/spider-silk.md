## Stronger than steel by weight
Dragline spider silk is tougher than steel of the same weight and can stretch far
before breaking. That combination of strength and stretch is hard to match with
any human-made fibre.

## Many silks, one spider
A single orb-weaver can make up to seven kinds of silk: stiff frame lines, sticky
capture spirals, soft egg-case wrapping, and more, each from a different gland.
[[orb weaver spider web]] [[spider spinning silk]]

## Spun as a liquid
Silk leaves the spider as a liquid protein and hardens into a solid fibre as it
is pulled, without heat or harsh chemicals, at room temperature.

## Webs as a sense organ
An orb web is an extension of the spider's senses. It reads the vibrations to
tell the difference between wind, a trapped fly, and a mate at the edge.
[[spider waiting web]] [[fly caught in web]]

## Flying on silk
Small spiders release fine strands and are lifted by air currents and even
atmospheric electric charge, a behaviour called ballooning that can carry them
for hundreds of kilometres.
[[spider ballooning silk]] [[spiderlings dispersing]]

## Hard to farm
Unlike silkworms, most spiders are territorial and cannibalistic, so silk cannot
be harvested at scale. Labs now brew silk proteins using engineered microbes
instead.
