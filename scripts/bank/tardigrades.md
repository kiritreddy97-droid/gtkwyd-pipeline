## They are everywhere
Tardigrades, also called water bears, are microscopic animals living in moss,
soil, beaches, and deep sea sediment on every continent. You have almost certainly
walked past millions of them.
[[moss macro close up]] [[tardigrade microscope]]

## They switch themselves off
When their surroundings dry out, tardigrades pull in their legs, lose almost all
their body water, and enter a state called a tun. Their metabolism drops to a tiny
fraction of normal and can stay that way for years.
[[dried moss microscope]] [[tardigrade tun state]]

## They survived space
In a 2007 experiment, tardigrades were exposed to the open vacuum of low Earth
orbit and to unfiltered solar radiation. Some survived and went on to reproduce
back on Earth.
[[low earth orbit satellite]] [[earth from space]]

## Extreme temperatures barely register
In the lab, tardigrades in the tun state have endured temperatures close to
absolute zero and brief exposure well above the boiling point of water.
[[frost ice crystals]] [[liquid nitrogen vapor]]

## They protect their own DNA
Tardigrades produce special proteins that shield their cells and genetic material
from damage as they dry out. Researchers are studying these proteins as a way to
keep fragile biological materials stable at room temperature.
[[dna helix model]] [[laboratory research microscope]]

## Toughness has limits
Tardigrades are not indestructible. In their active, hydrated state they are
fragile and easily killed. Their superpower is not strength, it is knowing when to
shut down completely.
