## The image lands upside down
The lens flips the picture, so it hits the back of your eye inverted. Your brain
has always corrected it, so you never notice.
[[human eye macro]] [[eye anatomy diagram]]

## There is a hole in your vision
Where the optic nerve leaves the eye there are no light receptors, creating a
blind spot in each eye. Your brain fills it in with a guess based on the
surroundings.
[[optic nerve illustration]] [[eye close up iris]]

## Only the centre is sharp
Just a tiny central patch of your retina sees fine detail. Everything else is
low-resolution. Your eyes dart around several times a second to build a detailed
scene.
[[eye looking around]] [[reading text close up]]

## You are partly blind while moving your eyes
During each quick eye movement, called a saccade, vision is briefly suppressed so
you do not see a smear. You lose a small slice of every day this way.
[[eyes scanning]] [[motion blur lights]]

## Colour vision is uneven
Most colour-sensing cells are packed in the centre and there are very few for
blue. Much of your rich colour impression is reconstructed by the brain.
[[colorful scene]] [[color wheel]]

## Your eyes are always cleaning themselves
Blinking, which you do around 15 to 20 times a minute, spreads a tear film that
keeps the surface smooth enough to see clearly.
