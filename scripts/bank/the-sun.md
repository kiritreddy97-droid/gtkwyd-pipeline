## Almost all the mass
The Sun holds about 99.9 percent of the mass in the entire solar system.
Everything else, all the planets, moons, and asteroids, is the leftover 0.1
percent.

## A slow journey out
Energy made by fusion in the core bounces between particles for tens of thousands
of years before it reaches the surface. From there, sunlight takes just over
eight minutes to reach Earth.
[[sun glowing plasma]] [[sunlight through space]]

## Not on fire
The Sun does not burn like a campfire. It fuses hydrogen into helium under
crushing pressure, converting about four million tonnes of mass into energy every
second.
[[solar flare eruption]] [[sun full disk]]

## A middling star
The Sun is often called average, but it is brighter than around 85 percent of
stars in the galaxy, most of which are small, dim red dwarfs.
[[star field milky way]] [[bright sun sky]]

## It has weather
The Sun flings out storms of charged particles. When a big one hits Earth it can
disrupt satellites and power grids and light up the polar skies.
[[solar wind illustration]] [[aurora from space]]

## A defined future
In about five billion years the Sun will swell into a red giant, likely
swallowing the inner planets, then shed its outer layers and fade to a white
dwarf.
