## The Moon pulls unevenly
The Moon's gravity is slightly stronger on the side of Earth facing it and
slightly weaker on the far side. This stretch raises the ocean into two bulges.
[[ocean tide coming in]] [[moon over sea]]

## Why two high tides a day
As Earth rotates, most coasts pass through both bulges each day, giving roughly
two high tides and two low tides in just over 24 hours.
[[tidal flats low tide]] [[harbor high and low tide]]

## The Sun helps too
The Sun raises smaller tides. When Sun and Moon line up, the effects add for
extra-large spring tides. When they are at right angles, tides are weaker neap
tides.
[[spring tide beach]] [[sun and moon sky]]

## Geography makes the range
The open ocean tide is only about half a metre. Funnel-shaped bays amplify it.
The Bay of Fundy in Canada sees a range of over 15 metres.
[[bay of fundy tide]] [[extreme tidal range coast]]

## Tides slow Earth down
Friction from the moving water gradually brakes Earth's spin and lets the Moon
drift outward. Days are slowly getting longer because of it.
[[waves on rocks]] [[earth moon system illustration]]

## Not just water
The solid ground also flexes up and down by about 30 centimetres twice a day, and
even the atmosphere has tides.
