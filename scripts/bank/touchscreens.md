## A grid you cannot see
Most modern touchscreens use a transparent layer with a fine grid of electrodes
running across the glass, carrying a tiny, constant electrical charge.
[[phone screen close up]] [[touchscreen electrode grid illustration]]

## Your finger is a conductor
The human body naturally holds a small electrical charge. When your finger
touches the glass, it draws a tiny amount of charge away from that grid at
that exact spot.
[[finger touching screen]] [[hand holding phone]]

## Triangulating the touch
Sensors around the edges measure the change in charge at multiple points on
the grid simultaneously and calculate exactly where the disturbance was
strongest, pinpointing your finger's location many times a second.
[[circuit sensors phone edge]] [[touch response animation]]

## Why gloves usually fail
Ordinary gloves insulate your finger, blocking the tiny charge transfer the
screen needs to sense. Touchscreen gloves work because they weave a
conductive thread into the fingertip.
[[gloved hand phone]] [[touchscreen gloves fingertip]]

## Multiple fingers at once
Because the whole grid is scanned continuously, the screen can track several
separate touch points at the same time, which is how pinch to zoom and
multi-finger gestures work.
[[pinch zoom gesture]] [[two fingers on screen]]

## A different method for styluses
Precision styluses often use a separate technology entirely, sometimes with a
tiny chip in the tip that talks directly to the screen, allowing far finer
detail than a fingertip ever could.
