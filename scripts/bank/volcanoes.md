## Most eruptions are underwater
The longest volcanic feature on Earth is the mid-ocean ridge, a chain running
around the globe on the sea floor. The vast majority of lava erupts there,
quietly, out of sight.

## They build islands
Hawaii, Iceland, and the Galapagos are all volcanic. Lava piles up layer by layer
from the sea floor until it breaks the surface as new land.
[[lava flowing into ocean]] [[volcanic island aerial]]

## A hotspot stays put
The Hawaiian islands form a line because the plate drifts over a fixed plume of
hot rock. Each island is older than the one before as it moves away from the
source.
[[hawaiian islands chain]] [[volcano erupting hawaii]]

## Ash can change the weather
A large eruption can inject sulphur high into the atmosphere, reflecting sunlight
and cooling the whole planet by a fraction of a degree for a year or two.
[[volcanic ash plume]] [[hazy sunset volcano]]

## Lightning in the plume
Ash particles rubbing together build up charge, so big eruptions often generate
their own lightning storms inside the column.
[[volcanic lightning]] [[eruption at night]]

## They made the air and oceans
Over billions of years, gases released by volcanoes supplied much of the water
vapour and carbon dioxide that formed early oceans and atmosphere.
