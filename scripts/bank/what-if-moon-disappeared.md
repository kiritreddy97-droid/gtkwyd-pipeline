## The tides would shrink
The Moon causes most of our ocean tides. Without it, the Sun would still raise
small tides, but they would be roughly a third of what we see now.
[[ocean tide coastline]] [[full moon over sea]]

## Coastal life would change
Many creatures time feeding, mating, and migration to the tides. Weaker, simpler
tides would disrupt those rhythms in tide pools and estuaries around the world.
[[tide pool creatures]] [[shorebirds mudflats]]

## Nights would go dark
A full Moon is the brightest natural light in the night sky. Remove it and
clear nights would be lit only by stars, changing the behaviour of nocturnal
animals.
[[dark starry night landscape]] [[nocturnal animal night]]

## Earth's tilt would drift
The Moon's steady pull helps hold Earth's axis near its current tilt of about
23 degrees. Without that anchor, the tilt could wander more over long periods,
making the climate less stable.
[[earth axis tilt diagram]] [[changing seasons]]

## Days would stop lengthening
The Moon slowly drags on Earth and gradually lengthens our day. Take it away and
that braking effect largely stops.
[[earth moon system]] [[clock and calendar]]

## No total eclipses
Total solar eclipses happen because the Moon appears almost exactly the same
size as the Sun. With no Moon, that rare and dramatic alignment would simply not
exist.
