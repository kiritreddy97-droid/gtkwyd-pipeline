## An eight minute delay
Sunlight takes just over eight minutes to reach Earth. If the Sun stopped
shining, we would keep seeing normal daylight, and feeling its gravity, for
those eight minutes.
[[sun bright sky]] [[solar system illustration]]

## Then darkness
After that, the sky would go dark except for stars and planets. The planets
would still be lit briefly by light already on its way, then fade.
[[dark sky planets]] [[earth in darkness space]]

## Orbits would go straight
With the Sun's gravity gone, Earth would stop curving around it and fly off in a
straight line into space at about 30 kilometres per second.
[[planet drifting space]] [[orbital path diagram]]

## The surface would freeze
Air temperatures would drop below freezing within a week and keep falling. The
top of the oceans would ice over, though the deep sea would stay liquid for a
long time, warmed from below by Earth's own heat.
[[frozen ocean surface]] [[icy landscape]]

## Some life could hang on
Around deep-sea vents and underground, ecosystems that never used sunlight could
survive for a while on Earth's internal heat and chemistry.
[[hydrothermal vent deep sea]] [[cave ecosystem]]

## Why it cannot actually happen
Stars do not just switch off. The Sun changes over billions of years, giving
any planet enormous warning. This is a thought experiment, not a forecast.
