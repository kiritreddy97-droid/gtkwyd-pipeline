## Sunlight is every colour at once
White sunlight is a mix of all visible colours, each with a different wavelength.
Blue and violet have short wavelengths, red and orange have long ones.
[[prism splitting light]] [[blue sky clouds]]

## Air scatters blue light most
Molecules in the air deflect short wavelengths far more strongly than long ones.
So blue light gets bounced around the sky in every direction, and that scattered
blue is what you see when you look up.
[[clear blue sky sun]] [[sunbeam through trees]]

## Then why not violet
Violet is scattered even more than blue, but sunlight contains less violet, and our
eyes are more sensitive to blue. The result our brain settles on is sky blue.
[[bright daytime sky]] [[sun high in sky]]

## Sunsets travel through more air
When the Sun is low, its light passes through a much longer slice of atmosphere.
The blue is scattered away completely before it reaches you, leaving the reds and
oranges to come straight through.
[[orange sunset horizon]] [[sun setting over ocean]]

## Dust and smoke deepen the colour
Particles from fires, volcanoes, or pollution scatter light too, which is why some
of the most vivid red sunsets follow large eruptions or wildfires.
[[hazy red sunset city]] [[volcanic ash sky]]

## On the Moon the sky is black
Without air there is nothing to scatter sunlight, so the lunar sky stays black even
in full daylight, and the Sun is a harsh white point.
[[moon surface black sky]] [[astronaut lunar landscape]]
