## Two coils, not a beam
Inside the charging pad is a coil of wire. Inside your phone is a matching coil.
Neither one sends power through open air the way a flashlight sends light.
[[phone on wireless charger]] [[charging pad close up]]

## Electricity makes magnetism
Running current through the pad's coil creates a changing magnetic field around
it. That field expands and collapses many thousands of times a second.
[[magnetic field illustration]] [[electronics circuit]]

## The phone's coil catches it
When your phone's coil sits inside that shifting magnetic field, the field
induces its own electric current in the phone's coil. That current charges the
battery.
[[phone charging screen]] [[coil diagram illustration]]

## Why alignment matters
The two coils need to sit close and lined up, which is why a phone charges
fastest sitting flat and centred on the pad, and barely charges if it slides to
the edge.
[[phone centered on charger]] [[misaligned charging pad]]

## Some energy always escapes
This method is less efficient than a cable, since some magnetic energy never
reaches the phone's coil and turns into heat instead, which is why wireless
charging feels warmer.
[[phone charging warm]] [[thermal camera image]]

## The same idea, bigger
The exact principle, called electromagnetic induction, was described in the
1830s and is also how some electric toothbrushes and even some electric cars
charge today.
