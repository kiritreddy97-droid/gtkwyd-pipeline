## Three hearts, blue blood
An octopus has three hearts. Two pump blood through the gills, and the third
sends it around the rest of the body. That heart actually stops beating when the
octopus swims, which is why they would rather crawl.
[[octopus swimming underwater]] [[octopus crawling on seabed]]

## A brain in every arm
About two thirds of an octopus's neurons are not in its head. They are spread
through its eight arms, so each arm can taste, touch, and react on its own,
even after it is separated from the body.
[[octopus tentacles close up]]

## They taste with their skin
The suckers on an octopus are covered in chemical receptors. When an octopus
reaches into a dark crack in the reef, it is not just feeling around. It is
tasting everything it touches.
[[octopus suckers macro]] [[coral reef crevice]]

## Masters of disguise
Special cells called chromatophores let an octopus change colour in under a
second. Some species also change the texture of their skin to match rock, sand,
or seaweed, despite being colour blind.
[[octopus camouflage]] [[mimic octopus]]

## Houdini of the sea
An octopus has no bones. The only hard part of its body is its beak, so if its
beak fits through a gap, the entire animal can squeeze through. Aquariums have
lost octopuses down drainpipes the width of a coin.
[[octopus squeezing through gap]]

## Short but brilliant lives
Most octopuses live only one to two years. They are among the most intelligent
invertebrates on Earth, and yet almost everything they know, they figure out
alone in a single short lifetime.
