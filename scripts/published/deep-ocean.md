## The deepest point is nearly 11 kilometres down
The Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench reaches about 10,935 metres. If you
dropped Mount Everest into it, the peak would still be more than two kilometres
underwater.

## Fewer visitors than the Moon
Twelve people have walked on the Moon. As of the early 2020s, only a handful of
people had reached the bottom of the Challenger Deep in person.
[[submersible deep sea]] [[astronaut on moon]]

## The pressure is crushing
At the bottom of the trench the pressure is over 1,000 times what you feel at sea
level, like balancing a small car on your thumbnail. Special vehicles need
thick, carefully engineered hulls.

## It is not lifeless
Even at the greatest depths there are amphipods, sea cucumbers, and microbes.
Some organisms there feed on chemicals seeping from the sea floor rather than on
sunlight-based food.
[[deep sea creatures bioluminescent]] [[hydrothermal vent]]

## Most of the sea floor is unmapped in detail
We have detailed maps of the surfaces of Mars and the Moon, but only a fraction of
Earth's sea floor has been mapped at high resolution. Much of it is better known
from rough satellite estimates.
[[ocean floor sonar map]] [[research vessel sonar]]

## Sunlight runs out fast
Below about 200 metres there is not enough light for photosynthesis. Below
1,000 metres it is completely dark, and many animals there make their own light.
