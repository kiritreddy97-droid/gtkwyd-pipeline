## The anomaly
In the 1930s, a mathematician discovered that most numbers in real-world datasets don't follow the expected pattern of 1/9, 2/9, 3/9, and so on. Benford's law reveals a hidden code in the way we represent numbers.

## The reason behind it
The digits 1, 2, and 3 are more likely to appear in the lead digit because of the way we record numbers. Fraudsters, on the other hand, often use fake numbers that follow a different pattern.

## The catch
As the numbers get bigger, the anomalies become more pronounced, making it easier to spot fake numbers. Detectives and auditors use Benford's law to uncover financial and scientific fraud.
