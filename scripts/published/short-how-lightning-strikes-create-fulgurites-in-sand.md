## The strike
Can you imagine the temperatures of 30,000°C that are created when a lightning bolt hits sand at speeds of 270,000 km/h? This channel can reach temperatures of up to 30,000°C, hotter than the surface of the sun.

## The formation
As the air channel hits the sand, it creates a cavity that can be up to 10 meters long and 1 meter wide. The sand grains around the cavity are melted and fused together, creating a glassy tube called a fulgurite.
[[fulgurite in the sand]] [[glassy tube]]

## The fossil
Fulgurites are essentially fossils of lightning bolts, preserving the shape and structure of the air channel. They can provide valuable information about the conditions under which the lightning strike occurred.
