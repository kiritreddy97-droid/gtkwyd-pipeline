## The setup
In 1969, astronauts planted a series of retroreflectors on the moon's surface. These reflectors use laser light to measure the moon's distance from Earth.
[[laser beam hitting retroreflector]] [[moon's surface]]

## The math
The reflectors bounce back a beam of light that's measured on Earth. By calculating the time delay, scientists can calculate the moon's distance.
[[laser beam returning]] [[calculator]]

## The result
The retroreflectors have been used to measure the moon's distance with incredible accuracy. The moon is about 239,000 miles (384,000 kilometers) away from Earth.
