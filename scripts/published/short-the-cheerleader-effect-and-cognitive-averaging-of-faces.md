## The pattern
When you look at a group of faces, can you spot the one that's actually the most average? This is known as the cheerleader effect.
[[cheerleader smiling]] [[group of faces]]

## Why it happens
Our brains are wired to recognize and respond to faces that are average or neutral. This is because faces that are too extreme or unusual can be perceived as threatening.
[[person looking at a face]] [[average face]]

## The flip side
This effect can also lead to cognitive averaging of faces, where we assume everyone looks like the average face. This can be a useful shortcut, but it can also lead to missed opportunities to notice individual differences.
