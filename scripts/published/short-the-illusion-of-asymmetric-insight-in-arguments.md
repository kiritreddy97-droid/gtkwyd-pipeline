## The asymmetry
When you're arguing, you tend to think you're more knowledgeable about the topic than the other person. This is because your brain is wired to recognize patterns and make predictions, but it's not always accurate.
[[argumentative person talking]] [[confused person looking at notes]]

## The cognitive bias
This phenomenon is known as the asymmetry of insight, where one person's expertise is perceived as more valuable than the other's. It's a classic example of the fundamental attribution error, where we overestimate the importance of our own knowledge.
[[expert talking confidently]] [[other person looking uncertain]]

## The illusion of advantage
The asymmetry of insight can lead to a false sense of advantage in arguments, causing us to become more entrenched in our positions. It's a subtle bias that can undermine our ability to listen to opposing views and find common ground.
