## The collective misremembering
In 2010, a viral phenomenon emerged when people claimed to remember Nelson Mandela, the former President of South Africa, dying in prison in the 1980s. This collective false memory sparked widespread debate and curiosity. The Mandela effect is a term coined to describe this phenomenon, where groups of people remember events or facts differently than what is recorded in history books.

## The psychology behind it
Research suggests that the Mandela effect may be due to a combination of factors, including confirmation bias, the influence of similar events, and the malleability of human memory. When people are exposed to conflicting information, their brains may struggle to reconcile the discrepancy, leading to the creation of false memories.

## The mystery remains
Despite numerous explanations, the Mandela effect remains a fascinating and unsettling phenomenon that challenges our understanding of perception, memory, and reality.
