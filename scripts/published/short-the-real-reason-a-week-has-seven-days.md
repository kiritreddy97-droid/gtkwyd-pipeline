## The Roman Influence
The seven-day week was actually introduced by the ancient Romans, who borrowed it from the Sumerians and Babylonians. They named the days after their own gods and goddesses.
[[Roman calendar]] [[ancient Roman architecture]]

## The Original Seven
The original seven-day week was 360 days, with an extra day added every four years to keep the calendar in sync with the solar year. This extra day was called the "intercalary day".

## The Julian Reform
In 45 BCE, Julius Caesar introduced the Julian calendar, which is the basis for the modern week. The seven-day week was retained, but with a leap year every four years to account for the extra day.
