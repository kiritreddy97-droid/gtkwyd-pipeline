## The plan
In 1944, the Allies used a river's sound to disrupt enemy aircraft during the Battle of the Rhine. They created a sonic boom by firing artillery shells underwater to create a series of underwater explosions.
[[explosions underwater]] [[sonar waves]]

## How it worked
The explosions created a pressure wave that traveled through the water and air, producing a sonic boom that could be heard by enemy aircraft. The Allies hoped the loud noise would be mistaken for enemy artillery fire, causing the planes to alter their course.
[[aircraft flying]] [[sonar screen]]

## The surprising outcome
The Rhine countermeasure was surprisingly effective, with reports of enemy aircraft being forced to change course or even crash. The Allies' use of subterranean acoustics became a key factor in the success of the Battle of the Rhine.
