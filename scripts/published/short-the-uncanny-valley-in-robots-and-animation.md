## The Valley of Discontent
As robots and characters get closer to human-like appearance, our emotional response shifts from fascination to revulsion. The more human-like a robot or character looks, the more we expect it to behave like a human, but it falls short.

## The Psychology of Discomfort
The uncanny valley theory suggests that our brains are wired to detect subtle differences between humans and non-humans, and when we're faced with something that's almost, but not quite, human, we feel discomfort. This discomfort can be intense enough to override our initial attraction to the robot or character.

## The Limit of Human-Like Design
While some robots and characters may have crossed the uncanny valley, others may still be stuck in it, forever trapped in a state of almost-but-not-quite human-likeness. This raises questions about the limits of human-like design and what we consider "human" in the first place.
