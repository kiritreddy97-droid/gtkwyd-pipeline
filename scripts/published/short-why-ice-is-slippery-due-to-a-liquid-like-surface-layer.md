## The Hidden Layer
When ice is exposed to air, a thin layer of water vapor forms on its surface. This layer is incredibly thin, but it's enough to make ice slippery.
[[thin layer of water vapor on ice]] [[person slipping on ice]]

## The Science Behind It
The water vapor layer acts like a liquid, allowing water molecules to slide past each other. This reduces the friction between the ice and any object that comes into contact with it.
[[water molecules sliding past each other]] [[person sliding on ice]]

## The Consequence
The slippery surface of ice is why we often see people wearing special shoes or using ice grips when walking on frozen surfaces.
