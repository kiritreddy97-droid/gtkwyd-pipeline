## It is a desert
A desert is defined by how little precipitation it gets, not by heat. Much of
inland Antarctica receives less than 50 millimetres of moisture a year, making it
the largest desert on the planet.

## It holds most of the world's fresh water
The Antarctic ice sheet contains roughly 60 percent of all fresh water on Earth. In
places the ice is over four kilometres thick.
[[antarctic ice sheet aerial]] [[glacier ice wall]]

## The land is pushed down by the ice
The weight of the ice is so great that it presses the bedrock hundreds of metres
below sea level in some regions. If the ice were removed, the land would slowly
rise back up over thousands of years.

## There are lakes under the ice
Hidden beneath kilometres of ice are hundreds of liquid lakes, kept unfrozen by
heat from the Earth and the insulating blanket of ice above. Lake Vostok is larger
than some countries' biggest lakes.

## No country owns it
The Antarctic Treaty, in force since 1961, sets the continent aside for peaceful
scientific use. Military activity and mineral mining are banned, and dozens of
nations cooperate on research there.

## It was once forested
Fossils show that many millions of years ago, when the continents sat differently
and the climate was warmer, Antarctica had forests and dinosaurs.
