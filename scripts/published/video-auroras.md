## It starts at the Sun
The Sun constantly streams out charged particles. Solar storms send extra bursts,
and when they reach Earth they interact with our magnetic field.
[[sun solar activity]] [[aurora green sky]]

## Funnelled to the poles
Earth's magnetic field deflects most particles but channels some down toward the
magnetic poles, which is why auroras form in rings around the far north and far
south.
[[earth magnetic field illustration]] [[aurora over snow]]

## The glow is excited air
The particles collide with gas atoms high in the atmosphere, kicking their
electrons to higher energy. As the electrons drop back, they release light.
[[aurora curtains night]] [[starry aurora landscape]]

## Colour is chemistry and altitude
Oxygen around 100 to 250 kilometres up glows green, the most common colour. Rarer
red comes from oxygen much higher. Nitrogen adds blues and purples lower down.
[[red and green aurora]] [[purple aurora]]

## The southern twin
The aurora australis lights up the sky around Antarctica at the same time, often
as a near mirror image of the northern display.
[[aurora australis]] [[aurora from space station]]

## Other planets have them too
Jupiter and Saturn have powerful auroras, and they have been detected on Uranus,
Neptune, and Mars, driven by the same basic physics.
