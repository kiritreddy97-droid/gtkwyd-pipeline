## Too small for GPS
A coin-sized tracker does not have room for a real GPS chip or a cellular
antenna. Instead it relies on a much simpler, much cheaper piece of tech,
Bluetooth.
[[bluetooth tracker device]] [[keys with tracker attached]]

## A quiet, constant signal
The tracker constantly broadcasts a short, encrypted Bluetooth signal, powerful
enough to reach only a short distance, roughly the size of a large room.
[[bluetooth signal illustration]] [[tracker on backpack]]

## Borrowing the crowd's phones
Any nearby smartphone from the same network, belonging to a total stranger,
can quietly detect that signal in the background and report an encrypted
location ping to the cloud, without that stranger noticing or ever seeing your
data.
[[crowd of people phones]] [[city street smartphones]]

## Stitched together into a map
Your own phone or a family member's checks the cloud, decrypts the pings using
a private key, and plots them to show roughly where the tracker, and whatever
it is attached to, last was.
[[phone app map location]] [[person checking phone map]]

## No signal, no update
If a tracker is somewhere with no nearby compatible phones, like a remote
field or a metal box, it simply cannot report a new location until it reaches
a phone again.
[[remote empty landscape]] [[tracker in bag]]

## Built with theft in mind
Modern trackers include safeguards like audible alerts if one is travelling
with someone who is not its owner, aimed at discouraging covert tracking of
people.
