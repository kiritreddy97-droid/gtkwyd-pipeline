## Water that eats rock
Rainwater picks up carbon dioxide from the air and soil, becoming a weak acid.
Over tens of thousands of years it dissolves limestone along cracks, widening
them into passages and chambers.

## Stalactites hang, stalagmites stand
Dripping mineral-rich water leaves tiny rings of calcite on the ceiling that grow
downward into stalactites. What lands on the floor builds stalagmites upward.
When they meet, they form a column.

## They grow painfully slowly
A stalactite often adds well under a millimetre a year. A formation the length of
your arm can represent tens of thousands of years of dripping.

## Some caves make their own weather
Large cave systems hold a near-constant temperature and humidity year-round, and
big ones can have their own air currents and even fog.
[[cave entrance mist]] [[vast cave chamber]]

## Life without light
Cave animals like blind fish and pale insects have lost their eyes and colour
over generations, since neither is useful in permanent darkness.

## The longest and deepest
Mammoth Cave in the United States has over 680 kilometres of mapped passages.
The deepest known cave, in Georgia, drops more than two kilometres.
