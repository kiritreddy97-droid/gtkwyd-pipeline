## Talking through the ground
Elephants make deep rumbles below the range of human hearing. These travel
through the ground as vibrations, and other elephants can detect them through
their feet from kilometres away.

## A trunk of 40,000 muscles
An elephant's trunk has no bone and an estimated 40,000 muscle units. It can
uproot a tree or pick up a single blade of grass.

## They know their dead
Elephants show clear interest in elephant bones and tusks, touching and turning
them with their trunks, and will revisit the place where a family member died.

## Cooling with their ears
African elephant ears are full of blood vessels. Flapping them on a hot day can
lower the blood's temperature by several degrees before it returns to the body.
[[elephant flapping ears]] [[elephant in heat savanna]]

## Built-in tumour defence
Despite their size and long lives, elephants very rarely develop tumours. They
carry about 20 copies of a protective gene that humans have only one pair of,
which helps damaged cells shut themselves down.

## Right or left tusked
Like people being right or left handed, elephants favour one tusk. The preferred
one wears down shorter and more rounded over time.
