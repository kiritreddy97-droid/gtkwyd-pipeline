## Not a photo
When you first scan your finger, the sensor does not save an image. It records
the position of ridge splits and endpoints and turns them into a mathematical
template.
[[phone fingerprint scanner]] [[fingerprint scan animation]]

## Three common sensor types
Capacitive sensors measure tiny electrical differences across ridges and
valleys. Optical sensors take a detailed light-based scan. Ultrasonic sensors,
found under some screens, bounce sound waves off your finger to map it in 3D.
[[fingerprint sensor under screen]] [[phone unlocking]]

## Matching, not storing images
Each time you unlock, the sensor builds a fresh template from your finger and
compares it mathematically to the stored one, checking for a close enough
match rather than a perfect pixel copy.
[[hand on phone scanner]] [[security lock icon]]

## Kept in a separate vault
On most modern phones that template is stored in an isolated chip, separate
from the main processor and the internet-connected apps, specifically so it is
hard to extract remotely.
[[smartphone chip close up]] [[secure chip illustration]]

## Why it sometimes fails
Wet, dry, or cut fingers change the ridge pattern the sensor reads, which is
why scanners sometimes ask you to try again or fall back to a passcode.
[[wet fingers phone]] [[phone passcode screen]]

## Not perfectly unique forever
Fingerprints can be subtly altered by scars, aging skin, or manual labour,
which is one reason phones let you register more than one finger.
