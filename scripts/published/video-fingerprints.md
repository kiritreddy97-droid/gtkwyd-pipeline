## Formed before birth
Fingerprint ridges develop in the womb, roughly between weeks 10 and 24, shaped by
the growth of skin layers and the pressure of amniotic fluid and finger
movement.
[[fingerprint close up]] [[hand of newborn baby]]

## Not fully genetic
Identical twins share DNA but not fingerprints. The exact pattern depends on
random physical conditions during development, so no two fingers anywhere match.
[[twins hands]] [[fingerprint scanner]]

## Koalas have them too
Koala fingerprints are so similar to human ones that they can be hard to tell
apart under a microscope. They likely evolved separately to help grip leaves and
branches.
[[koala gripping branch]] [[koala paw close up]]

## What they are for
Ridges are thought to improve grip on smooth or wet surfaces and to boost touch
sensitivity by channelling vibrations to nerves in the skin.
[[hand gripping glass]] [[textured surface touch]]

## They grow back the same
Minor cuts heal and the original pattern returns. Only deep scarring that
destroys the lower skin layer changes a print permanently.
[[healed finger]] [[magnifying glass fingerprint]]

## Three basic shapes
Almost every print is a loop, a whorl, or an arch, or a mix. Loops are the most
common, arches the rarest.
