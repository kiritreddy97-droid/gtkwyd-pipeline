## Glass that bends
Regular phone glass shatters if you try to fold it. Foldable phones use
ultra-thin flexible glass, often thinner than a human hair layered with
protective film, which can curve without snapping.
[[foldable phone bending]] [[thin flexible glass]]

## A hinge doing the hard work
The mechanical hinge is engineered to let the display curve in a wide, gentle
teardrop shape rather than a sharp crease, which spreads out the stress across
the fold.
[[phone hinge mechanism]] [[foldable phone open]]

## The crease you can feel
Even with a gentle curve, repeated folding does leave a visible or feelable
line over time, since the material is being flexed in the exact same spot
thousands of times.
[[foldable phone screen crease]] [[phone display close up]]

## Tested for years, not days
Manufacturers fold prototype hinges hundreds of thousands of times on
machines before release, simulating years of daily folding in a matter of
weeks.
[[robotic testing machine]] [[factory phone assembly]]

## Soft, not scratch proof
Because the top layer is flexible plastic or ultra-thin glass rather than
rigid glass, foldable screens scratch more easily than a normal phone screen,
which is why many ship with a protective layer already applied.
[[phone screen protector]] [[scratched screen close up]]

## Dust is the real enemy
Tiny grains of dust or sand getting into the hinge or under the folding
display are one of the most common causes of damage, which is why some
foldables use brush seals along the hinge edge.
