## The Earth's plates are always moving
The Earth's surface is divided into several large plates that float on the molten rock below. These plates are in constant motion, sliding over the asthenosphere, a layer of hot, viscous rock beneath the Earth's crust. The movement of these plates is the primary driver of mountain building, as the edges of the plates collide, pull apart, or slide past one another. The Himalayas, for example, were formed when the Indian plate collided with the Eurasian plate, causing the Earth's crust to buckle and fold. This process has been happening for over 50 million years, and it's still ongoing today.

As the Indian plate collides with the Eurasian plate, the Earth's crust is being stretched and thinned, a process known as continental extension. This causes the crust to thin and become more susceptible to faulting, which is why the Himalayas are experiencing such intense seismic activity.
