## Wings in a figure eight
Hummingbirds are the only birds that can truly hover and fly backwards. Their
wings trace a figure-eight, generating lift on both the forward and backward
stroke.

## Extreme heart rate
In flight a hummingbird's heart can exceed 1,200 beats per minute, and it may
take around 250 breaths per minute. At rest those rates drop sharply.
[[hummingbird feeding]] [[hummingbird perched]]

## Nightly shutdown
To survive cold nights without starving, many hummingbirds enter torpor, dropping
their body temperature and metabolism until they look almost lifeless until
dawn.

## Living on the edge of starvation
A hummingbird may visit over a thousand flowers a day and can starve within a few
hours. It remembers which flowers it has already emptied and when they refill.
[[hummingbird garden flowers]] [[hummingbird tongue]]

## A tongue like a pump
The forked tongue was long thought to work by capillary action. High-speed video
showed it actually springs open inside the flower to trap nectar, many times a
second.
[[hummingbird tongue close up]] [[hummingbird drinking nectar]]

## Epic migration
The ruby-throated hummingbird, weighing about as much as a coin, crosses the Gulf
of Mexico in a single non-stop flight of around 800 kilometres.
