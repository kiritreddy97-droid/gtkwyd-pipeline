## A punch like a bullet
The peacock mantis shrimp's club accelerates as fast as a small-calibre bullet.
It uses a spring-and-latch mechanism, storing energy and releasing it all at
once.

## Boiling water with a strike
The club moves so fast it creates collapsing vapour bubbles. Their collapse
releases a flash of heat and light and a second shockwave that also hits the
prey.
[[cavitation bubble illustration]] [[mantis shrimp burrow]]

## Eyes beyond ours
Human eyes have three types of colour receptor. The mantis shrimp has around a
dozen, plus the ability to detect polarised light, and each eye moves
independently.
[[mantis shrimp eyes close up]] [[mantis shrimp reef]]

## Depth perception in one eye
Each eye is split into three strips, so a mantis shrimp can judge distance with a
single eye, unlike animals that need two.
[[mantis shrimp head detail]] [[mantis shrimp hunting]]

## Armour that inspires engineers
The club survives thousands of strikes without shattering thanks to a spiralled,
energy-absorbing internal structure that materials scientists are copying.
[[mantis shrimp claw macro]] [[materials science lab]]

## Two kinds of hunter
Smashers use clubs to break shells. Spearers have barbed limbs to stab soft prey.
The two groups look and behave quite differently.
