## A volcano three times Everest
Olympus Mons rises about 22 kilometres, roughly two and a half times the height
of Mount Everest, and its base is about the size of the country of Poland.
[[olympus mons mars]] [[mars surface volcano]]

## A canyon that would span a continent
Valles Marineris stretches over 4,000 kilometres, long enough to reach across the
United States, and in places it is seven kilometres deep.
[[valles marineris mars]] [[mars canyon aerial]]

## The rusty colour is iron
Mars looks red because its dust is rich in iron oxide, essentially rust. Fine
dust coats almost the entire surface.
[[red martian landscape]] [[mars from orbit]]

## Two lumpy little moons
Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos, that look more like asteroids.
Phobos is slowly spiralling inward and will likely break apart into a ring in
tens of millions of years.
[[phobos moon mars]] [[mars and moons]]

## Sunsets are blue
Fine dust scatters light differently than Earth's air. During the day the Martian
sky is butterscotch, but around the setting Sun it turns a cool blue.
[[mars blue sunset rover]] [[mars rover horizon]]

## It once had rivers and lakes
Dried river channels, deltas, and lake beds show that liquid water flowed on the
surface billions of years ago, before Mars lost most of its atmosphere.
[[ancient mars river delta]] [[mars dry riverbed]]
