## It is measurably moving away
Mirrors left on the Moon by Apollo astronauts let scientists bounce lasers off the
surface and measure the distance precisely. The Moon recedes by about 3.8
centimetres each year, roughly the rate your fingernails grow.
[[full moon night sky]] [[apollo lunar surface]]

## Tides are the cause
Earth's rotation drags its tidal bulge slightly ahead of the Moon. That bulge pulls
the Moon forward, nudging it into a wider orbit, while the same effect slowly slows
Earth's spin.

## Days used to be much shorter
Because Earth's rotation is braking, days are lengthening by a tiny amount each
century. In the age of the dinosaurs a day was closer to 23 hours, and far earlier
it was under 19 hours.

## Total eclipses are a lucky coincidence
Right now the Moon appears almost exactly the same size in our sky as the Sun,
which is why total solar eclipses work. As the Moon retreats, that perfect match
will eventually be lost.
[[total solar eclipse]] [[moon crossing sun]]

## It steadies our seasons
The Moon's gravity helps keep Earth's tilt stable at around 23 degrees. Without it,
the tilt could wander much more, making the climate far less predictable.
[[earth axis tilt illustration]] [[changing seasons landscape]]

## Nothing to worry about
The drift is extraordinarily slow. It would take billions of years to matter, and
the Sun will change dramatically long before the Moon gets truly far away.
