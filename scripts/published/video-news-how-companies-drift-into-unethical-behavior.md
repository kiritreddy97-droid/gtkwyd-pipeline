## A gradual shift
Companies often believe that unethical behavior emerges suddenly, but new research from ESMT Berlin shows that it can be a gradual process. Employees can collectively drift into misconduct as groups gradually reshape what they perceive as ethical or acceptable.

## The power of collective perception
The study highlights the importance of individual perceptions in shaping organizational culture. As a group of employees becomes more homogeneous in their views, they can inadvertently create an environment that fosters unethical behavior.

## A gradual escalation
The research suggests that this gradual shift can occur over time, with employees becoming more accepting of certain behaviors as they become more normalized. This can lead to a point where the group as a whole is engaging in misconduct without even realizing it.

## The role of social norms
The study emphasizes the role of social norms in shaping organizational behavior. As employees internalize the norms of their group, they can become more willing to engage in behaviors that were previously considered unacceptable.

The research highlights the difficulty of changing the culture of an organization once it has drifted into unethical behavior. It requires a concerted effort from leadership and employees to recognize and address the issue before it becomes too deeply ingrained.

## The importance of awareness
