## Relative humidity matters
You may think you know how smoke particles transform, but a new study by Md. Sohel Rana and Marcelo I. Guzman challenges this assumption. Sohel Rana and Marcelo I. Guzman at the University of Kentucky found that the amount of water in the air can significantly affect how smoke particles transform after dark. This is a surprising discovery, as it suggests that something as familiar as humidity can have a major impact on air quality.

## Catechol transformation
The researchers focused on catechol, a phenolic compound associated with biomass burning. When smoke particles containing catechol are exposed to air, they undergo a process called oxidation, which breaks down the compound into different chemicals. However, the rate and outcome of this transformation depend on the relative humidity in the air.

## Chemical pathways redirect
The study showed that relative humidity can redirect the chemical pathways involved in the nighttime oxidation of catechol. This means that if the air is too dry, the oxidation process may occur more quickly, while high humidity can slow it down. This has important implications for understanding how smoke pollutants affect air quality and human health.

## Implications for air quality
The findings of this research highlight the importance of considering humidity when studying the effects of smoke pollutants. By understanding how humidity influences the transformation of catechol, scientists can better predict the impact of biomass burning on air quality and develop more effective strategies for mitigating its effects.
