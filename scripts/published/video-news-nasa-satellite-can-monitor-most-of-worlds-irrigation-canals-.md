## A global network of waterways
Millions of kilometers of human-made canals crisscross the world, carrying water from lakes, rivers, and reservoirs to farmland. These canals form a sort of circulatory system for global agriculture, supplying water to crops and helping to feed millions of people.
[[canals on map]] [[satellite image of canals]]

## Limited monitoring capabilities
Despite their importance, many agricultural areas lack the means to monitor the ebb and flow of water through these waterways. This makes it difficult for farmers and water managers to make informed decisions about water use.
[[gauges and inspections]] [[local reports]]

## A new way to monitor canals
A recent study has found that a NASA satellite can monitor most of the world's irrigation canals. This new technology could help to improve water management and reduce the reliance on scattered gauges, inspections, and local reports.
[[NASA satellite image]] [[satellite data visualization]]

## Benefits of improved monitoring
By monitoring the canals more effectively, farmers and water managers can better manage water resources, reduce waste, and optimize crop yields. This could lead to more efficient use of water and improved food production.
[[water management map]] [[agricultural productivity graph]]

## A more sustainable future
The ability to monitor irrigation canals more effectively could also help to reduce the environmental impact of agriculture. By identifying areas where water is being wasted or overused, farmers and water managers can take steps to reduce their ecological footprint.
