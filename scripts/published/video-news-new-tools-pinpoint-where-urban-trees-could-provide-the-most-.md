## A problem with broad targets
Cities often set broad tree canopy targets, but these targets can be misleading. They might not account for the fact that not all shade is equally useful, and not every neighborhood has room for more trees.
[[city street with few trees]] [[tree canopy map]]

## Shade is not always equal
The amount of relief a tree can provide from heat depends on various factors, such as the tree's size, shape, and location. A single tree might provide more shade in some areas than others, making it difficult to determine which trees will have the greatest impact.
[[tree with varying shade]] [[tree in different locations]]

## Neighborhoods vary in tree space
Not every neighborhood has room for more trees. Urban areas often have limited space, and the type of land use can affect the feasibility of planting trees. For example, areas with heavy foot traffic or limited parking might not be suitable for tree planting.
[[city street with heavy foot traffic]] [[park with limited space]]

## New tools can help
New tools and technologies are being developed to help cities pinpoint where urban trees could provide the most relief from heat. These tools can analyze data on tree density, canopy cover, and other factors to identify areas that would benefit most from tree planting.
[[tree density map]] [[canopy cover analysis]]

## A more targeted approach
By using these new tools, cities can develop more targeted approaches to urban tree planting. This could lead to more effective heat relief strategies and a better use of limited urban space.
