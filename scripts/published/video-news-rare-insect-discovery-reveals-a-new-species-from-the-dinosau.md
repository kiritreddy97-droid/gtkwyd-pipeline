## A tiny insect from the dinosaur age
Some 99 million years ago, during the age of the dinosaurs, a tiny insect became trapped in tree resin. The resin hardened and turned into amber, preserving the insect's fossil. This process has allowed scientists to study the insect in incredible detail.
[[tree resin]] [[amber]]

## A new species discovered
A team of researchers from the School of Zoology and The Steinhardt Museum of Natural History has identified and described a new species: Cretodorus noonoo, or "Noo-Noo." This discovery is significant because the insect was previously unknown to science.
[[insect fossil]] [[museum exhibit]]

## The insect's fossil was preserved for millions of years
The fossilized insect has been preserved for an astonishing 99 million years, providing scientists with a unique opportunity to study its characteristics and learn about its evolution.
[[fossilization process]] [[amber fossil]]

## A new species with a unique name
The new species, Cretodorus noonoo, has been given a unique name: "Noo-Noo." This name reflects the insect's unusual characteristics and its discovery in the "Noo" region.

## A significant discovery for the scientific community
The discovery of Cretodorus noonoo is a significant one for the scientific community, as it provides new insights into the evolution of insects and the diversity of life on Earth.
