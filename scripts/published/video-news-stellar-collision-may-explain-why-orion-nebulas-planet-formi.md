## A stellar collision
Your eyes are on a cosmic collision that's been hiding a key to understanding the universe, involving 1 Ori C1 and the Orion Nebula. The central star, 1 Ori C1, is surrounded by a disk of gas and dust in which planets form.

## Radiation strips away material
Radiation from the massive central star, 1 Ori C1, gradually strips material from the planet-forming disks, giving them shapes that resemble tadpoles. This process can affect the disks' appearance and potentially influence the formation of planets.

## The disks' appearance
The planet-forming disks in the Orion Nebula appear younger than their stars, which is unusual. The disks' shapes, caused by the radiation from the central star, resemble tadpoles.
[[disk shapes]] [[tadpole shape]]

## The central star's impact
The massive central star, 1 Ori C1, plays a significant role in shaping the planet-forming disks. Its radiation affects the disks' material, leading to the tadpole-like shapes.

## The Orion Nebula's unique environment
The Orion Nebula is a large star-forming region visible to the naked eye. The presence of young stars surrounded by planet-forming disks makes it an interesting region to study.

## The mystery remains
Despite the radiation from the central star, the exact reason why the planet-forming disks appear younger than their stars remains a mystery. Further study is needed to understand this phenomenon.
