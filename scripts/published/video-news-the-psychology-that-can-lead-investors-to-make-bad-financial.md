## Why do we blame others when we lose?
Do you ever find yourself saying "I made a good call" when you're really not sure? But if the trade had lost money, they might be less quick to blame their own judgment. This is because we tend to be more critical of ourselves when we fail.

## The problem of self-deception
This is a problem of self-deception. When we make a profit, we tend to overestimate our own abilities and underestimate the role of luck and circumstance. We might say things like "I made a good call" or "I was lucky." But when we lose, we might blame external factors like "the market was jittery" or "I was just having a bad day."

## The role of cognitive bias
Cognitive bias plays a big role in this phenomenon. We tend to be biased towards a positive self-image, which means we tend to focus on our successes and ignore our failures. This can lead us to overestimate our own abilities and make poor decisions in the future.

## The importance of self-awareness
Being aware of these biases and tendencies is important for making good financial decisions. It's not about being perfect, but about being aware of our own limitations and being willing to learn from our mistakes.
[[psychology can lead investors make]]

## The need for humility
Humility is also important when it comes to making financial decisions. We need to be willing to admit when we don't know something and be open to new information and perspectives. This can help us make better decisions and avoid the pitfalls of self-deception.
