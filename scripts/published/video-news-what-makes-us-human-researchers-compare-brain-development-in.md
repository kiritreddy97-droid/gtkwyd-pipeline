## How much bigger is the human brain
The human brain is significantly larger than that of our closest evolutionary relatives, the chimpanzees and gorillas. But exactly how much bigger is it, and what are the key differences in brain development between these species?
[[brain comparison]] [[monkey brain]]

## No new genes, just different usage
Researchers found that the human brain became larger not because of new genes, but because of how humans and non-human apes use the genes they share. This suggests that the key to brain development lies in the way genes are expressed, rather than the number of genes themselves.
[[gene expression]] [[DNA molecule]]

## Brain organoids for comparison
To compare brain development in humans, chimpanzees, and gorillas, an international team used brain organoids grown from stem cells. These tiny, three-dimensional brain structures can be grown in the lab and can mimic the development of the brain in early life.
[[brain organoids]] [[stem cells]]

## International team compared brain development
The team, led by Prof. Mareike Albert at TUD Dresden University of Technology, compared brain development in humans, chimpanzees, and gorillas. They used brain organoids to study how these species develop their brains, and what differences they found.
[[research team]] [[Prof. Mareike Albert]]

## Brain development is a complex process
Brain development is a complex process that involves many different genes and mechanisms. The team's study suggests that understanding how these genes are used and expressed is key to understanding how the human brain became so much larger than that of our closest evolutionary relatives.
[[brain development]] [[complex process]]

## Future research directions
The team's study provides new insights into brain development and evolution. Future research could build on these findings to better understand how the human brain became so much larger, and what the implications of this are for our understanding of human evolution.
