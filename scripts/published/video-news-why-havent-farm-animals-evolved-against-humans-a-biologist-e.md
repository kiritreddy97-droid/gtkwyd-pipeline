## A 15-year-old biologist wonders
Farm animals have been interacting with humans for 15 years, but what's the real reason they haven't evolved to protect themselves? A 15-year-old from India asks this question, sparking curiosity about why animals can't defend themselves against their human handlers.

## Humans are a constant presence
Farm animals live in close proximity to humans, which means they are constantly exposed to human activity, noise, and stress. This constant presence may make it difficult for animals to develop strong defensive mechanisms against humans.

## Evolution takes time
Evolution is a slow process that occurs over many generations. It requires significant changes in an animal's behavior, physiology, or anatomy, which can take thousands of years to develop. Given the relatively short time humans have been interacting with farm animals, it's unlikely that animals have had enough time to evolve strong defensive strategies.

## Animals have adapted to their environment
Farm animals have adapted to their environment in other ways. For example, some animals have developed behaviors to avoid humans, such as fleeing or hiding. Others have learned to rely on humans for food and shelter.

## The benefits of domestication outweigh the costs
Domestication has provided farm animals with benefits such as food, shelter, and protection from predators. While this comes at the cost of reduced defensive capabilities, the benefits of domestication may outweigh the costs for many animals.
