## Rivers follow gravity, not maps
North is just a direction we draw at the top of a page. Water flows from higher
ground to lower ground, and for the Nile that path happens to head north.
[[nile river aerial]] [[river winding landscape]]

## The land slopes down toward the sea
The Nile begins in the highlands of East Africa, around 1,000 metres or more
above sea level, and ends at the Mediterranean. Over its length the ground drops
steadily northward.
[[east african highlands]] [[nile delta from space]]

## Two rivers in one
The White Nile starts near Lake Victoria and provides a steady flow. The Blue
Nile begins in Ethiopia and delivers most of the water and nearly all the silt
during the summer rains.
[[blue nile falls ethiopia]] [[confluence of rivers]]

## The flood built a civilisation
For thousands of years the annual flood spread fertile silt across the valley.
Egyptian farming, and the calendar, were organised around it until modern dams
ended the cycle.
[[egyptian farmland nile]] [[ancient egypt illustration]]

## A ribbon of green in the desert
Seen from orbit, the Nile is a thin green thread through the tan Sahara. Almost
everyone in Egypt lives within a few kilometres of it.
[[nile valley satellite]] [[green fields desert edge]]

## Other rivers flow north too
The Nile is not unique. Several major rivers run northward, and dozens of smaller
ones do, wherever the terrain slopes that way.
