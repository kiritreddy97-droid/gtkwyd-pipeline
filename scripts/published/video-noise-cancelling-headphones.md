## Sound is a wave
Noise is just air pressure moving up and down in a pattern. If you could create
the exact opposite pattern at the exact same time, the two would cancel out.
[[headphones close up]] [[sound wave visualization]]

## A tiny built-in microphone
Active noise-cancelling headphones have microphones that listen to the outside
world dozens of thousands of times per second, constantly sampling the noise
around you.
[[headphones microphone detail]] [[person wearing headphones]]

## An inverted copy, instantly
A chip flips that sound wave upside down and plays it through the speaker along
with your music. The flipped wave meets the real noise and the two flatten each
other out.
[[circuit board chip]] [[sound wave cancellation diagram]]

## Better on low, steady sounds
This trick works brilliantly on constant drones like an airplane engine or a fan,
but sudden sounds like a voice or a clatter are harder to predict and cancel.
[[airplane cabin interior]] [[person talking]]

## The physical seal still matters
The cushions blocking sound by simply sitting over your ear, called passive
isolation, actually removes a big share of noise before the electronics do
anything at all.
[[headphone ear cushion]] [[over ear headphones]]

## A trade-off in your ears
Because the circuitry is always listening and reacting, some people notice a
faint pressure feeling when noise cancelling is switched on. Turning it off
removes that instantly.
