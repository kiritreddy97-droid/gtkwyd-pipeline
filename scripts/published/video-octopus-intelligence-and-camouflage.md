## Can it change its skin in 1/50th of a second?
Did you know that octopuses can squeeze through openings as small as 1/4 inch in diameter, making them escape artists like no other? This is made possible by the unique structure of their skin, which is covered in tiny, pigmented cells called chromatophores. When an octopus wants to change colour, it contracts or relaxes these cells to expand or contract the skin's pigment, allowing it to change its skin colour in a matter of milliseconds. In fact, some research suggests that an octopus can change its skin colour in as little as 1/50th of a second, making it one of the fastest colour changes in the animal kingdom.

## A distributed brain
Unlike humans, who have a centralized brain, octopuses have a distributed brain, with a large proportion of their neurons found in their arms. This means that each arm can function independently, allowing the octopus to solve problems and make decisions without needing to send signals all the way back to its central brain. In fact, some research suggests that an octopus's arms can even make decisions without the octopus's central brain even knowing about it.
