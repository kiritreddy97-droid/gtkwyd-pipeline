## It is graphite, not lead
Pencil cores are made of graphite, a form of carbon. The confusion goes back to
the 1500s, when people thought a newly found deposit in England was a kind of
lead ore.
[[pencil tip close up]] [[graphite mineral]]

## One English mine started it all
Pure, solid graphite good enough to cut into rods came from a single deposit in
Borrowdale, England. It was so valuable it was guarded and controlled by the
crown.
[[english countryside hills]] [[old graphite mine]]

## Wars forced an invention
Cut off from English graphite, a French officer named Nicolas-Jacques Conte mixed
powdered graphite with clay and baked it. Changing the ratio changes the
hardness, which is still how it works.

## Why pencils are yellow
In the 1800s the best graphite came from China. An American maker painted its
pencils yellow, a colour linked with royalty in China, to signal quality, and
the look stuck.

## Hexagonal for a reason
Round pencils roll off desks and are harder to grip. The six-sided shape stays
put and uses wood efficiently when cut from a block.
[[hexagonal pencils pattern]] [[pencil on desk]]

## A very long line
A single average pencil is often said to be able to draw a line tens of
kilometres long, or write tens of thousands of words, before it runs out.
