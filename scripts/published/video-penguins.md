## Deep divers
Emperor penguins routinely dive past 200 metres and have been recorded below
500, holding their breath for over 20 minutes by slowing their heart rate and
tolerating low oxygen.

## Huddles that rotate
In an Antarctic blizzard, emperor penguins pack into huddles where the inside can
reach room temperature. Birds on the cold edge slowly shuffle around so everyone
takes a turn in the middle.

## Fathers do the incubating
The male emperor penguin balances the single egg on his feet under a flap of skin
for about two months of winter, eating nothing, while the female feeds at sea.
[[male penguin with egg]] [[penguin colony winter]]

## Counter-shaded for the hunt
A penguin's black back and white belly is camouflage. From above the dark back
blends with the deep water, from below the pale belly blends with the bright
surface.

## Salt glands
Penguins drink seawater. A gland above the eyes filters out the salt, which drips
off the tip of the beak.
[[penguin head close up]] [[penguin shaking head]]

## Only one lives at the equator
Almost all penguins live in the southern hemisphere, but the Galapagos penguin
lives on the equator, cooled by cold ocean currents.
