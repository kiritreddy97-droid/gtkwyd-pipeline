## A necessity, not a luxury
Humans and livestock need salt to survive, and before refrigeration it was the
main way to preserve meat and fish. Controlling salt meant controlling food.
[[salt crystals close up]] [[salt harvesting flats]]

## Paid in salt
Roman soldiers received an allowance connected to salt, and the Latin word for it,
sal, is the root of the English word salary.
[[roman soldiers illustration]] [[ancient coins]]

## Cities and roads followed it
Salzburg means "salt city". Ancient trade routes like the Via Salaria in Italy
existed specifically to move salt inland from the coast.
[[old salt trade route]] [[historic european town]]

## Taxed and fought over
Governments have taxed salt for millennia. A hated salt tax in France helped fuel
revolution, and Gandhi's 1930 Salt March protested a British salt monopoly in
India.
[[salt march historical photo]] [[protest march crowd]]

## Mined and evaporated
Salt is dug from ancient dried-sea deposits underground or made by letting
seawater evaporate in shallow ponds under the sun.
[[underground salt mine]] [[seawater evaporation ponds]]

## Now cheap and everywhere
Industrial production made salt so cheap that most of it today is used not for
food but for de-icing roads and for the chemical industry.
