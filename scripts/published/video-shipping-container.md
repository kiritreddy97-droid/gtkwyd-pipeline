## Loading ships used to take days
Before containers, cargo was loaded piece by piece by gangs of dockworkers. A
ship could spend as long in port being loaded as it spent at sea.
[[old dock workers cargo]] [[vintage harbor ship]]

## One man standardised it
In the 1950s, American trucking businessman Malcolm McLean pushed the idea of a
standard steel box that could move straight from truck to ship to train without
being unpacked.
[[container being lifted crane]] [[container ship port]]

## The savings were staggering
Container handling cut the cost of loading cargo by well over 90 percent. Goods
that were once too expensive to ship long distances suddenly were not.
[[stacked containers yard]] [[gantry crane loading]]

## Standard sizes made it work
The whole system depends on containers being the same dimensions worldwide, so
any crane, truck, and ship can handle any box.
[[container yard aerial]] [[containers on train]]

## It hollowed out some ports and built others
Old city docks that could not fit huge container terminals declined. New deep-
water ports and the towns around them boomed.
[[modern container terminal]] [[abandoned old docks]]

## Millions are at sea right now
At any moment, tens of millions of containers are moving around the planet,
carrying most of the manufactured goods in world trade.
