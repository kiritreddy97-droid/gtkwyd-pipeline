## One plant, many teas
Green, black, white, and oolong tea all come from the leaves of Camellia
sinensis. The difference is how much the picked leaves are allowed to oxidise
before drying.

## Oxidation is the key step
Bruised tea leaves react with oxygen and turn darker, like a cut apple. Green tea
is heated quickly to stop this. Black tea is left until fully oxidised.

## A legend and a long history
Chinese tradition credits a mythical emperor with discovering tea thousands of
years ago. Solid evidence of tea drinking in China goes back well over 2,000
years.
[[chinese tea ceremony]] [[ancient tea house painting]]

## It reshaped global trade
Demand for tea in Britain drove trade with China, the rise of fast clipper ships,
and the spread of tea plantations across India and Sri Lanka under colonial
rule.
[[clipper ship sea]] [[indian tea estate]]

## Afternoon tea is a recent invention
The custom of a light meal with tea in the late afternoon dates only to the
1840s, credited to a British duchess who got hungry between lunch and a late
dinner.
[[afternoon tea spread]] [[teapot and cups]]

## The second most consumed drink
After plain water, tea is the most widely consumed beverage on Earth, ahead of
coffee and every soft drink.
