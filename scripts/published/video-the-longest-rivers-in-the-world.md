## The Nile is the longest river
Stretching over 6,695 kilometers from its source in Burundi to its delta on the Mediterranean Sea in Egypt, the Nile is widely considered the longest river in the world. Its length has been measured and re-measured over the years, with some estimates suggesting it could be as long as 7,000 kilometers, but the 6,695-kilometer mark is the most widely accepted figure. The Nile's length is so impressive that it's often used as a reference point in geography and cartography. It's also a vital source of water and transportation for millions of people in Africa, supporting agriculture, industry, and urban centers.

## The Amazon River is a close second
While the Nile is the longest river in the world, the Amazon River is the largest river by discharge volume, accounting for about 15% of the fresh water that flows into the oceans. Stretching over 6,400 kilometers through Brazil, Peru, and Colombia, the Amazon River is a massive waterway that supports an incredible array of plant and animal life. The Amazon River's sheer size and importance make it a vital component of the global ecosystem, and its length is often cited as one of the longest rivers in the world.
