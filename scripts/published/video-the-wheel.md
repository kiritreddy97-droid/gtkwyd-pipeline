## Later than you would guess
The wheel appears only around 3500 BC, thousands of years after agriculture,
pottery, sewing needles, and boats. It is not an obvious idea.
[[ancient wooden wheel]] [[old wooden cart]]

## The hard part is the axle
A rolling disc is easy. The real challenge is a fixed axle with a hole precise
enough to turn freely but tight enough to bear weight, which needs metal tools
and seasoned wood.
[[wooden wheel and axle]] [[cross section wheel hub]]

## It needs the right terrain
Wheels are useless in dense forest, soft sand, mountains, or marsh. Cultures in
such places sensibly relied on pack animals, sledges, and boats instead.
[[cart on dirt road]] [[pack animals mountain trail]]

## The Americas knew the principle
Ancient peoples in Mexico made wheeled toy animals but never used wheels for
work, most likely because they had no large draft animals to pull carts.
[[wheeled toy artifact]] [[ancient mesoamerican ruins]]

## The potter's wheel may be older
Some evidence suggests rotating wheels were used to throw pottery before they
were put under vehicles.
[[potter at wheel]] [[clay pot spinning]]

## The spoked wheel was a second leap
Early wheels were solid and heavy. Around 2000 BC, spoked wheels made chariots
light and fast, changing warfare across the ancient world.
