## No air pressure
On Earth, the weight of the atmosphere pushes down on water and keeps it liquid.
In space there is almost no pressure at all.
[[astronaut spacewalk]] [[water droplet floating]]

## It boils instantly
With nothing pressing on it, the water can no longer hold together as a liquid.
It flashes into vapour and spreads out in every direction.
[[boiling water bubbles]] [[space station window]]

## And freezes at the same time
Boiling carries away energy fast, so the vapour and any leftover droplets chill
below freezing almost immediately, forming a cloud of tiny ice crystals.
[[ice crystals macro]] [[frost forming]]

## Astronauts have seen it
When spacecraft dump waste water, crews describe a brief sparkling flurry of ice
outside the window, sometimes called a snow shower in orbit.
[[spacecraft in orbit]] [[sunlight through ice particles]]

## Inside the station it behaves
In the pressurised cabin, water is perfectly normal. It just forms floating
wobbling blobs because there is no gravity to pull it into a puddle.
[[floating water blob space]] [[astronaut with water]]

## Why it matters
Understanding how liquids behave without pressure or gravity is essential for
designing fuel tanks, life support, and cooling systems for spacecraft.
