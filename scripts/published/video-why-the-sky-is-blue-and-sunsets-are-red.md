## When sunlight meets tiny particles
When sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere, it encounters tiny molecules of gases such as nitrogen and oxygen. These particles scatter the shorter, blue wavelengths of light more than the longer, red wavelengths, a phenomenon known as Rayleigh scattering. This scattering effect is responsible for the blue colour we see in a clear sky during the daytime.
[[rayleigh scattering]] [[atmospheric gases]]

## The scattering effect is more pronounced
The amount of scattering that occurs depends on the size of the particles and the wavelength of light. Since blue light has a shorter wavelength, it is scattered more than red light, which is why the sky appears blue during the day. This effect is also why sunsets often appear red or orange, as the longer wavelengths of light are able to travel through the atmosphere with less scattering.
[[sunset colours]] [[atmospheric scattering]]

## Atmospheric conditions affect the colour
The colour of a sunset can also be influenced by atmospheric conditions such as dust, water vapour, and pollution. For example, during a severe dust storm, the red and orange hues of a sunset can be muted or even turned into a reddish-brown colour due to the presence of large particles in the air.
[[dust storm]] [[atmospheric pollution]]

## The angle of the sun affects the colour
The angle at which the sun is viewed also plays a role in determining the colour of a sunset. When the sun is lower in the sky, the light has to travel through more of the atmosphere, which scatters the shorter wavelengths of light and leaves mainly the longer wavelengths, such as red and orange, to reach our eyes. This is why sunsets often appear more vibrant and colourful when viewed from a lower angle.
[[sunset angle]] [[atmospheric optics]]

## The Earth's atmosphere is constantly changing
The Earth's atmosphere is constantly changing due to weather patterns, seasonal variations, and human activities. These changes can affect the amount of scattering that occurs, which in turn can alter the colour of the sky and sunsets. For example, during a severe storm, the amount of dust and water vapour in the air can increase, leading to more scattering and a more muted colour.
